01 — Brand expression

Present, not prominent.

حولك means “around you.” The identity is about the people and checks that surround a patient after discharge — the pharmacist who confirms a line, the daughter who sees a missed reminder. The brand should feel like that support: there when needed, never louder than the instruction itself.

Precise

Every status means one thing. “Reported” never stands in for “verified.”

Unhurried

One task per screen for patients. White space is a reading aid, not decoration.

Accountable

Every instruction shows where it came from and who approved it.

Warm, not soft

Respectful adult language. No mascots, no reassurance the product can’t back up.

02 — Wordmark & monogram

Three routes, one recommendation.

hawlak
Route A · Wordmark only

Lowercase, medium weight, −0.065em. The “k” leg is shortened 4% so it doesn’t out-reach the “h” ascender; “wl” spacing is opened 2 units to stop the counters merging at small sizes.

Honest and quiet, but has no app-icon or favicon form.

Selected
hawlak
Route B · Open ring + wordmark

A 320° ring around a solid point: the person, and the people around them. The 40° opening at one o’clock keeps it from reading as a target or a lock, and points toward the wordmark. Dot is drawn 6% larger than geometric so it holds equal weight against the ring.

Ring 5.2 / dot r6.4 on a 48 grid · clear space = dot diameter.

حولكhawlak
Route C · Arabic-first bilingual

حولك leads at 600 weight; the Latin sits right-aligned beneath at 24% of the Arabic cap height. No added tracking or kashida on the Arabic — the baseline of “ك” anchors the lockup.

Strongest local signal; awkward for Uzbek, Urdu and English-first users.

Why B

The ring scales down to a 16px favicon and a lock-screen notification, which A can’t do. It carries the meaning of the name without leaning on any one script, so it works the same for Arabic, Uzbek and English users. C’s Arabic lockup is kept as the localized version of B, not discarded.

Neutral rendering for content design. Final appearance is controlled by WhatsApp and the user’s device.

TemplatesReminders are business-initiated, so each is a pre-approved utility template per language. A patient reply opens a 24-hour window for follow-up messages from the care team.
ButtonsThree quick replies — the platform maximum — each kept under 20 characters in Arabic, English and Uzbek. “I need help” is last so it’s never the default thumb position.
PrivacyThe first line — what shows on a lock screen — is only “Evening reminder · 8:00 PM”. Medicine names appear after opening, never in the notification preview.
VoiceA voice note leads; the same words follow as text, so the reminder still works in a quiet room or for someone who prefers reading. Patients can reply by voice too.
ProvenanceEvery reminder names the plan and the pharmacist who checked it. If a line is held, the message says one medicine is still being checked rather than leaving it out silently.
LanguageSet at discharge from the patient’s stated preference. Replying “language” (or لغة / til) sends a list message to change it. Medicine names are transliterated, not translated.
TruthfulnessThe confirmation says plainly that “I took it” is a report, not a check. Free-text questions about medicines are routed to a pharmacist, never answered automatically.
